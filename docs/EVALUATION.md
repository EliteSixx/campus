# CampusLink AI evaluation

This report is generated by `ai/eval/render_report.py` from `ai/eval/results.json`. The fixed seed is 42. All placement and application outcomes used here are synthetic.

## Method

- Pipeline matching mode: `embedding_plus_exact_and_alias`. Seeded random ranking uses one deterministic random score per application; the CGPA baseline ranks by CGPA only.
- Matching labels come from 4,671 rows of `applications.csv` across 60 jobs. ROC-AUC is pooled across application pairs. Precision@5 and Precision@10 are macro averages of the per-job values; within a job, precision uses the top min(k, application count).
- The generator signal is reconstructed by replaying the seed-42 random draws and verified against all saved shortlist labels; its formula and threshold source is `ai/data/generate_data.py:76-78 (formula line 76, threshold line 77, flip line 78)`.
- Eligibility correctness uses a seeded simple random sample of 5,000 unique pairs from the full 90,000-pair student/job Cartesian product. Ground truth is recomputed from CGPA, active backlogs, and eligible branches.
- Readiness outcomes compare the readiness service score to whether a student has any accepted offer. Monotonicity compares the original score with a 0.1 CGPA increase where possible and with one additional active backlog.
- JD parsing uses 20 descriptions written and labelled before the first parser run. Missing parser CGPA value `0` accompanied by the parser's `Minimum CGPA not specified` warning is treated as not stated (`null`) for scoring.
- Pipeline match, eligibility and ranking metrics used `embedding_plus_exact_and_alias`. Readiness itself is rule-based and does not use embedding matching. The skill-pair benchmark compares exact-plus-alias matching with the configured embedding model at cosine threshold 0.75.
- The related-skill threshold sweep compares the ten hand-labelled related pairs with the ten different pairs; same/alias pairs are excluded because the matcher handles them in its exact/alias pass first.
- Latency measurements ran directly against the service functions on the developer laptop recorded below; no HTTP or network request is included in timings.

The matcher beats the CGPA-only baseline on pooled ROC-AUC (0.5282 versus 0.5184). The report records this result as observed.

The deterministic pre-noise score has ROC-AUC 0.5469 against the final shortlisted labels; the realized noisy generator score has ROC-AUC 0.7841, an oracle upper bound under the generator's Gaussian noise and 17% flip.
The deterministic signal's ROC-AUC of 0.5469 is the realistic ceiling for any scorer because its Gaussian noise and 17% label flips are random and unobservable; the 0.784 oracle uses the hidden draw and is not a reachable target.
The matcher retains 60.15% of the deterministic signal's above-chance AUC ((matcher AUC - 0.5) / (deterministic AUC - 0.5)). The oracle share (67.37%) is reported only as a diagnostic; the noisy signal against pre-flip labels has ROC-AUC 1.0000.

## Limitations

- The data is synthetic.
- There is partial circularity between the data generator and the matcher: shortlist outcomes depend partly on required-skill overlap and CGPA, signals that the matcher also uses. About 17 percent of shortlist outcomes were flipped.
- The JD and skill-pair hand-labelled sets are small and were written by the team.
- There are no real placement outcomes in this evaluation, so these figures are not real-world accuracy.
- The figures show that the pipeline works and is internally consistent; they do not establish real-world accuracy.
